# Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on RTX 4090 and RTX 3090: make decode not wait on the bus

**Status:** plan, not a record. No number here was measured for this page; every figure is either
quoted from [`qwen4_exp_performance.md`](../../performance/qwen4_exp_performance.md) with the row it
came from, or derived from the released configs (the derivation is shown so it can be checked).

**Target:** `--backend qwen4_exp` decoding at a usable rate on **8× RTX 4090 (`sm_89`)** and **8×
RTX 3090 (`sm_86`)**, against today's 3.6 tok/s.

## What is actually binding, in the tree's own measurement

The performance record's decode section is the starting point and it is unusually complete:

| Scenario (4×2080 Ti) | ms/token | Ceiling |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Serial H2D + compute | 138.6 | 7.2 tok/s |
| Perfect H2D/compute overlap | 103.6 | 9.6 tok/s |
| + 31% resident cache, uniform routing | 71.5 | 14.0 tok/s |
| INT8 experts | 51.8 | 19.3 tok/s |
| **INT8 + 62% resident** | 35.0 | **28.6 tok/s** |
| Any scheme with no expert H2D | 35.0 | 28.6 tok/s |

The per-token byte count is the bound: top-10 of 512 experts, round-robin ownership, 48 layers = 120
expert copies = **1.099 GiB a rank a token**, 103.6 ms of H2D against **35.0 ms** of measured compute.
And the record says plainly that **28.6 tok/s is a compute number, not a transfer number** — nothing
done to the expert pipeline passes it.

So the whole plan is one sentence: **remove the H2D, then find out what the compute floor is on a
4090 and a 3090, which is not the 2080 Ti's 35.0 ms.**

## Why eight cards is what makes that possible, and four was not

The record's residency table is computed for **four 22 GiB cards**, and it is why the model was
host-staged at all:

| Expert dtype | Total | Per rank (TP4) | + dense | Fits 22 GiB |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| BF16 | 229.7 GiB | 57.4 GiB | 60.0 GiB | no |
| INT8 | 112.5 GiB | 28.1 GiB | 30.7 GiB | no |
| Q4_K-ish (4.5 bit) | 63.3 GiB | 15.8 GiB | 18.4 GiB | yes |

**The FP8 release changes the third column without any new quantization work, and eight cards changes
the divisor.** Derivation, from the released configs:

- `hidden_size` 2560, `moe_intermediate_size` 640, 512 experts, 48 layers → one expert is
  `3 × 2560 × 640` parameters; at 2 bytes that is **9.375 MiB**, and `× 512 × 48` is **225.00 GiB**,
  which is the record's "~225 GiB" to the byte.
- `Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-FP8` declares `quant_method: fp8`, `weight_block_size: [128, 128]`, and
  943 `modules_to_not_convert` entries — the dense projections, norms, router, `lm_head` and
  `embed_tokens` stay BF16; **only the routed experts and the PLE n-gram table are quantized**.
- So the expert set is `225.00 / 2 = ` **112.50 GiB**, and the published checkpoint totals
  **172.82 GiB** against BF16's 335.30 GiB (both read off the repos' own file listings).

At **TP8** that is **3072 experts a rank × 4.688 MiB = 14.06 GiB**, plus ~1.27 GiB of dense (half the
TP4 figure) and the ~2 GiB the record allows for KV and activations: **~17.3 GiB of 24 GiB**, with
headroom. On four 22 GiB cards the same arithmetic gives 28.1 GiB a rank and does not fit — which is
exactly the record's INT8 row.

**Eight cards is the difference between "staged over PCIe" and "resident".** Both target boxes have
eight 24 GiB cards.

## The mechanism already exists

`HostExpertMoE` holds an LRU keyed `(layer_idx, expert_id)` whose capacity is the
`--expert-cache` option, and `HostExpertMoE._fetch` stages from the host only on a miss. The option's
own help says what the default means: **`0` restages every step**. So full residency is
`--expert-cache 3072` at TP8 — a launch flag, not new code, until a measurement says otherwise.

The runtime also already reads the FP8 checkpoint: `models/qwen4_exp/quant.py` (`FP8Tensor`,
`dequantize_block_fp8`), `weights.py::load_layer_fp8` and `_detect_fp8`, and a `HostExpertShard` that
allocates codes **and** block scales as one group so a staging copy never straddles a pinned and an
unpinned half. Its docstring names the constraint the plan has to respect: *"Only the codes and their
128x128 block scales cross PCIe — half the bytes of BF16 — and the dequantization happens on the
device right before the GEMM."* That is also why FP8 pays on **both** cards even though only one has
FP8 tensor cores.

## The work, in order

**W0 — stage the FP8 checkpoint.** 172.82 GiB from ModelScope, which is half the BF16 pull. This is
the long pole and it is running.

**W1 — does it serve at all on `sm_89`?** The fatbin is `7.5;8.9`, so the 4090 is covered by the
existing build; nothing about this card is new kernel work. What is new is that no Qwen3.8-Flash-Next
run has happened on it. Acceptance: a served request returns tokens, and the QSA path — whose MMA arm
is behind `__CUDA_ARCH__ >= 750`, so `sm_89` takes it — is checked rather than assumed.

**W2 — full residency, and the number it buys.** Serve with the expert cache sized for every
`(layer, expert)` this rank owns, and compare against the same run at `--expert-cache 0`. The
instrument is the runtime's own counters — `HostExpertMoE.stage_bytes` and `stage_calls` are the H2D
traffic, and a resident run should drive them to zero after the first pass — plus `relicllm bench`
for TTFT/TPOT/throughput. **This is the measurement the whole plan exists to take.**

**W3 — whatever W2 says is binding next.** If residency lands, the next bound is compute, and the
record's own ranked list for decode applies: NCCL (8.8 ms of the 35.0 ms floor, 96 small
all-reduces), the `linear_attention` dense projections (11.2 ms), full attention (8.8 ms). Those
percentages are 2080 Ti numbers and will not transfer; they are the list of places to look, not a
prediction. Note also that TP8 doubles the ranks in every collective, which may move NCCL the wrong
way — worth measuring before assuming.

**W4 — `sm_86` for the 3090.** The fatbin is `7.5;8.9` and does not contain `8.6`, and a missing
gencode is not a slow path but `no kernel image is available for execution on the device` at the
first launch. This is a `relic-core` build change (the arch list plus its three doc mirrors), the same
shape as the `7.5;8.9` change that brought up the 4090. The 3090 has **no FP8 tensor cores** — `sm_86`
is below the `major >= 8` fork `relic_core` uses for its fp8 impl — so on that card FP8 weights are
dequantized before the GEMM. Residency still pays there (14.06 GiB of codes is what makes them fit,
and the dequantization of the active experts is device-local), but the compute floor is a different
number and has to be measured rather than inherited.

## Acceptance

A served decode rate on the 4090 and on the 3090, each measured with `relicllm bench` and each
recorded with the launch flags it was taken at, against the 3.6 tok/s that is today's number — plus
the `stage_bytes` reading that says whether the H2D is gone. A run that reports a rate without the
staging counter cannot distinguish "resident" from "a bigger LRU", and the difference is the point.

## What this plan does not claim

- **It does not claim 28.6 tok/s.** That is the 2080 Ti's compute floor. A 4090's is higher and a
  3090's is a different number again, and neither has been measured for this model.
- **It does not claim FP8 is free.** The record's ceiling table assumes quantized experts; it does not
  measure the accuracy cost of this particular FP8 build on this model, and the model's parity guard
  is the thing that would say.
- **It does not claim the released FP8 checkpoint is the right artifact** for the 3090 — that is what
  W4 measures. If it is not, a 4-bit expert format is the record's own ranked first lever, and it is a
  much larger piece of work than this plan.
